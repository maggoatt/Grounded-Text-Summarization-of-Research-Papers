For the Layer Specialization Hypothesis (H1), we observed significant and robust differences in activation patterns between recall and reasoning tasks across many layers. The Attention Head Specialization Hypothesis (H2) extends the notion of functional differentiation to the granularity of individual attention heads, proposing that specific heads preferentially engage in recall or reasoning tasks. Distributions of these features were compared between recall and reasoning tasks using the Mann-Whitney U test, and effect sizes were quantified with Cohen's d. To control for multiple comparisons, FDR correction was applied with α = 0.01. A head was defined as specialized if it exhibited significant differential activation between recall and reasoning tasks in at least three of these metrics while also satisfying the statistical and effect size thresholds. To test H3, the activations of individual neurons within the MLP layers were analyzed.
